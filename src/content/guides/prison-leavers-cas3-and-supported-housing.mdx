A stable address on release is one of the strongest protections against reoffending. It is also one of the hardest things to arrange, and supported housing providers often take people at short notice with limited information.

## The Community Accommodation Service

HMPPS runs the Community Accommodation Service in three tiers.

| Tier | What it is | Who it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CAS-1 | Approved Premises with 24-hour staffing | People assessed as high risk, usually as a licence condition |
| CAS-2 | Accommodation for people on bail or home detention curfew | People who could be released early or bailed if they had an address |
| CAS-3 | Up to 84 nights of temporary accommodation with basic floating support | Prison leavers at risk of homelessness, including people leaving CAS-1 or CAS-2 |

CAS-3 launched in five probation regions in July 2021 with £19.8 million of funding and has since been rolled out across England, delivered by contracted providers and, in some areas, through council-led arrangements.

## The duty to refer

Since October 2018, prisons, youth offender institutions and probation services have had a duty to refer people they think may be homeless or at risk of homelessness to a council of the person's choice, with their consent. The council then assesses what homelessness duties it owes.

## Where exempt accommodation fits

When CAS-3 ends, many people move into exempt accommodation, especially shared supported houses. Done well, that gives people a settled base with support. Done badly, it concentrates people with offending histories in a few streets, with little support and weak links to probation.

The Levelling Up, Housing and Communities Committee heard evidence in 2022 about exactly this pattern, and councils now pay close attention to where prison leavers are placed.

<Callout type="watch" title="Risk information">
Never accept a prison leaver without the risk information you need to keep them and other residents safe. Ask probation for the risk assessment, licence conditions, any exclusion zones and whether MAPPA applies. Record what you asked for and what you were given.
</Callout>

## A referral checklist

1. Confirm the release date, the probation practitioner's name and contact details, and the licence conditions.
2. Get risk information, including risks to staff, residents, children and known victims.
3. Check whether exclusion zones or curfews affect the property you are offering.
4. Check compatibility with current residents, including any known associations or conflicts.
5. Start the Housing Benefit and Universal Credit claims on or before the day of release.
6. Agree how you and probation will share information about concerns or breaches.
7. Complete the needs assessment and support plan within the first weeks, and in any case within four weeks.

## Good practice

- Keep a named contact at the local probation office and meet regularly.
- Agree a protocol for licence breaches and recalls, including what happens to the room and the claim.
- Plan support around the person's goals as well as their risks: work, family contact, health and recovery.
- Track outcomes, including whether people stay, move on in a planned way or return to custody.
