faqs:
  - q: How long should someone stay in transitional housing?
    a: Long enough to be ready to sustain a home and no longer. Most commissioned schemes set a target of six months to two years and review it with the resident. Stays that drift on with no plan are a common audit finding.
  - q: Should residents in transitional housing have a tenancy or a licence?
    a: It depends on the facts, not the label. If the resident has exclusive possession of their own self-contained space, it is likely a tenancy. Shared rooms, staffed hostels and accommodation where staff need unrestricted access are more often licences. Since the Renters' Rights Act took effect, the choice also changes which possession grounds apply.
  - q: What is the shared accommodation rate exemption for hostel residents?
    a: Single people under 35 renting privately normally get the lower shared accommodation rate of Local Housing Allowance. People under 25 who have spent at least three months in a homeless hostel, and care leavers under 25, can claim the one-bedroom rate instead. They usually need to tell the DWP or council themselves.
  - q: What counts as a positive move-on?
    a: A planned move into settled accommodation the person chose and can afford, with any support they still need in place. Evictions, abandonments and moves into another crisis service are not positive outcomes, even if the bed is freed up.

Transitional housing sits between crisis and a settled home. Someone might arrive from a hostel, a hospital ward, a prison or a sofa, and leave for a social tenancy, a private rented flat or a long-term supported setting. The building matters less than what happens while they are there.

## What makes housing transitional

Three things separate transitional housing from long-term supported housing:

1. The stay has an expected length, agreed with the resident and reviewed.
2. The support is aimed at independence, so it should reduce over time.
3. Someone is tracking where people go when they leave.

If none of these is true, the scheme is long-term housing with a transitional label. That is not wrong in itself, but it should be planned and funded as long-term housing.

## Typical lengths of stay

Commissioned schemes often set targets between six months and two years. Young people's schemes and foyers tend to run up to two years because they include education and work goals. Step-down schemes for people leaving hospital can be much shorter.

The national picture is that stays get longer when move-on housing is scarce. A resident who is ready to leave but has nowhere to go is not a support failure, but it should still be recorded, because it is evidence a commissioner or council can act on.

## Tenancy or licence

The agreement you use affects possession, repairs and the resident's rights. The label on the document does not decide it. Courts look at whether the resident has exclusive possession of their space.

| Setting | Usually |
| --- | --- |
| Shared room or staffed hostel with communal facilities | Licence |
| Own room in a shared house, staff access limited to what the agreement allows | Often a tenancy |
| Self-contained flat with own front door | Tenancy |

Since 1 May 2026 the Renters' Rights Act has changed private assured tenancies, including in supported housing run by landlords that are not registered providers. Licences are outside those reforms. Our [Renters' Rights Act guide](/guides/renters-rights-act-supported-accommodation/) covers the new supported accommodation grounds for possession.

<Callout type="watch">
Calling an agreement a licence does not make it one. If a resident has exclusive possession of a self-contained flat, a court is likely to treat it as a tenancy, with the notice rules that come with that.
</Callout>

## A move-on plan that works

The best transitional services treat move-on as part of support from day one. A plan that starts in month eleven is usually too late.

1. At the first support session, ask where the resident wants to live and what has gone wrong with past tenancies.
2. Check eligibility early: local connection, immigration status, rent arrears from previous tenancies and any housing register bans.
3. Get the resident on the housing register and any local move-on schemes in the first month.
4. Build tenancy skills that match the destination, such as budgeting for bills in a private let or dealing with a letting agent.
5. Line up any support that needs to continue after the move, such as floating support or a care package, before the move date.
6. Record the outcome when the resident leaves, and check in at three months if you can.

## Money at the point of move-on

Rent in supported housing is usually paid by Housing Benefit, while rent in the next home may be paid by the Universal Credit housing element. The switch can leave a gap of several weeks.

- Help the resident start or update the Universal Credit claim before the move, not after.
- Ask about advance payments and local welfare assistance for furniture and deposits.
- For under-25s, check the shared accommodation rate exemptions. People who have spent three months or more in a homeless hostel, and care leavers, can claim the one-bedroom rate.

## What to record

Councils and commissioners are asking for outcome data, and the national standards proposed under the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023 include support that is planned around each person. Keep a record for every resident of:

- the date they arrived and the date they left
- the move-on plan and each review
- where they moved to, and whether the move was planned
- whether support continued after the move

This record is also what makes a scheme credible when it applies for a licence or a commissioning contract.
