## Monday 26 October: what I do now

I audit resident support files in supported exempt accommodation.

Licensing is expected from 2027. The planned licence conditions include a needs assessment for every new resident within four weeks of moving in, and councils will want to see that the support in the plan actually happened.

Most providers believe their files would pass. Very few have had them checked by someone independent.

Here is a typical example. Licence agreement signed and dated. Needs assessment current. A plan for every scored risk. Weekly support hours met and recorded. Then a 6-month review, completed 15 days after it was due.

One late review. That file is Amber, not Green. It is fixable, and it is exactly what an inspector would find first.

## Wednesday 28 October: the Competence and Conduct Standard

If you are a Registered Provider, the Competence and Conduct Standard applies to you from this month, and that includes your supported housing.

The short version:

- All relevant staff must have the skills, knowledge, experience and behaviours to deliver good housing services.
- Senior housing managers need a level 4 qualification. Senior executives need a level 5.
- The deadline is October 2029 for providers with 1,000 or more homes, and October 2030 for smaller ones.
- If a managing agent delivers housing management for you, its staff can be in scope too.

Charities and CICs that are not registered providers are outside it. Their staff will be tested through council reviews and licensing instead.

## Friday 30 October: a free support plan template

A support plan is the document I check hardest in an audit, because everything else in the file should link back to it.

So I have made a free one. It is a Word document with:

- up to four goals, in the resident's own words
- first steps for the next two weeks
- who gives what support, how often and where
- risk and move-on sections
- a review log and a session record sheet

Add your logo and change anything you need. It is free to download.
