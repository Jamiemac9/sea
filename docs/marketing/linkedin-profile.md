## About

Paste as it is, then change anything that is not quite right.

> I audit resident support files in supported exempt accommodation.
>
> Licensing is expected from 2027. When it arrives, councils will ask whether each resident had a needs assessment within four weeks, whether their support plan matches their needs, and whether the records show the support happened. Most providers believe their files would pass. Few have had them checked by someone independent.
>
> That is what SEA Audit does. We review a sample of files, or every file on a regular cycle, and rate each one Gold, Green, Amber or Red. Every finding names the document and the date, and every gap comes with an example of what good looks like.
>
> My background is housing, property management and compliance, with qualifications across property and health and safety. I started SEA Audit because the same gaps kept appearing, and providers kept asking the same question: what does good actually look like?
>
> I also publish ExemptAccommodation.com, a free resource for the sector: plain-English guides, free templates, a regulation tracker and a readiness check that takes about ten minutes.
>
> I work with:
> - Registered Providers overseeing managing agents
> - Managing agents and support providers preparing for licensing
> - Landlords and investors leasing property to providers
>
> If you want to know how your files would hold up, message me or book a call at seaaudit.com.

## How to post

- Three posts a week: Monday, Wednesday and Friday at about 8am.
- Put the link in the first comment, not the post, and say "link in the comments" at the end.
- Reply to every comment in the first hour. Spend ten minutes before posting commenting on other people's posts in the sector.
- One idea per post. Short paragraphs. No hashtag walls: two or three at most.
- Use real examples from audits only with names and identifying details removed.
- Never post a resident's details or anything that could identify a scheme without permission.
