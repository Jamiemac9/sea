Awaab's Law sets fixed deadlines for social landlords to investigate and fix hazards in tenants' homes. Phase 1 started on 27 October 2025 and covers damp and mould and all emergency hazards. Phase 2 starts on 30 November 2026.

## Does it apply to supported housing?

It depends on two things.

1. **Who the landlord is.** Awaab's Law applies to registered providers of social housing.
2. **How the resident occupies the home.** The government guidance includes supported accommodation occupied under a tenancy. It does not apply to any housing occupied under a licence.

Many exempt accommodation schemes use licence agreements, so many will be outside Awaab's Law for now. Check your actual agreements rather than the scheme description. The property still has to be safe either way: councils can act on hazards under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and the government plans to extend Awaab's Law to the private rented sector later.

## The new hazards from 30 November 2026

- Excess cold and excess heat
- Falls
- Structural collapse and falling elements
- Fire and explosion
- Electrical hazards
- Domestic and personal hygiene, including pests

## The timescales

| Hazard | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency | Investigate and make safe within 24 hours |
| Significant | Investigate within 10 working days, then make safe within 5 working days |
| Further work | Start within 5 working days, or within 12 weeks if that is not practicable |
| Written summary to the tenant | Within 3 working days of the investigation ending |

## Why this matters for managing agents

The deadlines start when the landlord becomes aware of a hazard. The guidance says that where a managing agent becomes aware, that is likely to count. So a resident telling a support worker their heating has stopped may already start the clock for the registered provider.

The practical fix is simple: every hazard mentioned in a support session gets logged and passed on the same day, with a date, a time and a repair reference. SEA Audit has a [guide to logging hazards in support records](https://seaaudit.com/resources/awaabs-law-support-records/).
