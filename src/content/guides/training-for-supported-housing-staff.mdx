faqs:
  - q: What training do supported housing support workers need?
    a: At minimum, fire safety, safeguarding adults (and children, if you house under-18s or families), first aid cover, data protection and the basics of your own policies. Most services add mental health awareness, drug and alcohol awareness, de-escalation and lone working, because those match residents' needs.
  - q: Is there a mandatory qualification for supported housing support workers?
    a: Not for frontline support workers in exempt accommodation. Registered providers must meet the Competence and Conduct Standard, which requires qualifications for senior housing managers and executives. CQC-registered care services expect new care workers to complete the Care Certificate.
  - q: Does the Competence and Conduct Standard apply to supported housing?
    a: It applies to registered providers of social housing, including their supported housing. Staff are in scope if managing the delivery of housing management services takes up most of their time or is a substantial part of their role. Charities and CICs that are not registered providers are not covered by it.
  - q: Do online training courses count?
    a: Online courses work well for knowledge, such as safeguarding, data protection and mental health awareness. Practical skills, such as first aid and using fire equipment, need hands-on training. Whatever the format, record what each person learned and check in supervision that they can apply it.
  - q: How often should safeguarding training be refreshed?
    a: The law does not set a period. Many services refresh safeguarding every one to three years, and sooner after a serious incident or a change in local procedures. Write your period into your training policy and stick to it.

Ask a council officer what they look for in a supported housing service and staff competence comes up quickly. Residents in exempt accommodation often have several needs at once, and the people supporting them need the skills to match. From 2027, licensing is expected to make this a formal test for service managers.

## Where the training duties come from

| Source | Who it applies to | What it requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire safety law | Every employer with staff on the premises | Fire safety training at induction and when risks change, repeated as needed, in working hours |
| First aid regulations | Every employer | Adequate first aid cover, based on an assessment of your needs |
| Competence and Conduct Standard | Registered providers of social housing, from October 2026 | Staff with the skills for their role, and qualifications for senior housing managers and executives |
| Learning disability and autism duty | Services registered with the CQC, since July 2022 | Training appropriate to each person's role |
| Supported accommodation regulations | Ofsted-registered accommodation for 16 and 17 year olds | Four quality standards, a workforce plan and rules on the fitness of staff |
| Supported housing licensing | Supported exempt accommodation, expected from 2027 | Licence holders to check service managers have suitable character, capacity, experience and skills |

## Fire safety training

Article 21 of the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 requires the responsible person to make sure staff get adequate safety training. It must happen when they start, again when they face new or increased risks, and be repeated where needed. It has to match the fire risk assessment and take place during working hours.

In supported housing, the fire risk assessment should reflect residents' needs. Staff should know each resident's personal emergency evacuation plan, what to do at night when fewer staff are on shift, and how to respond to fire setting or smoking in rooms.

## The Competence and Conduct Standard

The Competence and Conduct Standard applies to registered providers of social housing from October 2026. All relevant staff must have the skills, knowledge, experience and behaviours to deliver good housing services.

Qualifications are required for two groups:

- **Senior housing managers** need a level 4 qualification regulated by Ofqual.
- **Senior housing executives** need a level 5 qualification or a foundation degree.

Homeless Link reports that staff are in scope if managing the delivery of housing management services takes up at least half their time, or is a substantial part of their role. Large providers have until October 2029 for staff to qualify and smaller ones until October 2030.

<Callout type="watch" title="Using a managing agent">
If a managing agent delivers housing management for a registered provider, its staff can still be in scope. The provider has to hold its service providers to the same standard.
</Callout>

The standard does not apply to charities and community interest companies that are not registered providers. Their staff will be tested through licensing and council reviews instead.

## Care services and the CQC

Where a supported living service provides personal care, it must register with the Care Quality Commission. Since 1 July 2022, CQC-registered providers have had to train staff in learning disability and autism, appropriate to their role. The Oliver McGowan training is the government's preferred package, but equivalent training can meet the duty.

New care workers are expected to complete the Care Certificate, the induction standard for health and social care. It gained a sixteenth standard, on learning disability and autism, in 2025.

## Accommodation for 16 and 17 year olds

Supported accommodation for looked-after 16 and 17 year olds and care leavers aged 16 and 17 must register with Ofsted. Providers must meet four quality standards: leadership and management, protection, accommodation and support. The regulations also require a workforce plan and set rules on the fitness of staff. Expect inspectors to ask how staff are recruited, trained and supervised.

## A training matrix for a typical service

This is a starting point, not a legal list. Adjust it to your residents and your contracts.

| Training | Who needs it | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire safety and evacuation | Everyone | Legal duty |
| Safeguarding adults | Everyone | Expected by councils and safeguarding boards |
| Safeguarding children | Services with under-18s or families | Expected by councils and Ofsted |
| First aid | Enough staff to give cover on every shift | Legal duty to provide adequate cover |
| Data protection | Everyone who handles resident records | Expected by the ICO, and the records are sensitive |
| Mental health awareness | Support staff | Matches the needs of most residents |
| Drug and alcohol awareness, including overdose response | Support staff in services where residents use drugs | Matches residents' needs |
| De-escalation and lone working | Support and night staff | Staff safety |
| Professional boundaries and trauma-informed practice | Support staff | Quality of support |

## What councils and licensing will check

A certificate shows someone attended a course. Councils want to see that staff can do the job. In an audit, expect to be asked for:

1. A training matrix with dates and next due dates for every member of staff.
2. Induction records for new starters.
3. Supervision notes that discuss practice, not only caseloads.
4. Evidence that learning from incidents led to training or changes.
5. Service manager experience and qualifications, ready for the licensing test.

<Callout type="practice" title="Make the matrix do the work">
Keep the matrix where managers see it every week, with overdue courses at the top. A matrix that is only updated before an audit is easy to spot.
</Callout>
