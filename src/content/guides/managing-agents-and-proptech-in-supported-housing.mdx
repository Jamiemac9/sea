Managing agents and proptech companies are moving into supported housing fast. Investors want assets managed to a standard, providers want systems that save staff time, and councils want evidence. The opportunity is real, and so is the risk of building something that looks good and fails the first council review.

## Who carries which duty

Supported housing usually involves several organisations. Each has its own duties, and some duties can sit with more than one of them.

| Role | Typical duties |
| --- | --- |
| Freeholder or investor | Lease terms, structural repairs if the lease says so |
| Landlord, such as a charity or housing association | Occupancy agreements, rent setting, Housing Benefit evidence, property safety, licensing from 2027 |
| Support provider | Needs assessments, support plans, support delivery and records, safeguarding |
| Care provider, in supported living | CQC registration, personal care, care records |
| Managing agent | Whatever the management agreement says, often repairs, compliance checks, voids and rent collection |

<Callout type="watch" title="Delegating the work is not delegating the duty">
A landlord can ask an agent to arrange gas checks, but if a check is missed, the landlord is still in breach. Agents can also become liable in their own right as the person managing or in control of an HMO. Agreements should set out tasks, timescales, reporting and what happens when something is missed.
</Callout>

## For managing agents

A management agreement in supported housing should cover at least:

1. The properties covered and the services the agent provides.
2. Compliance tasks, with timescales and who pays.
3. How repairs are reported, prioritised and tracked, with response times.
4. Void management, including how quickly rooms are made ready and who decides who moves in.
5. Reporting to the landlord: what, how often and in what format.
6. Access to properties and how residents' privacy and safety are protected.
7. Data sharing, including what the agent may see about residents.
8. What happens at the end of the agreement, including handover of records.

## For proptech teams

Supported housing has a different centre of gravity from general lettings. The questions that matter are about people and evidence, so the data model has to start there.

### The data points that matter most

| Data point | Who asks for it |
| --- | --- |
| Move-in date and needs assessment date | Licensing (assessment within four weeks), councils |
| Support plan and review dates | Councils, commissioners, licensing |
| Support sessions with time, duration and content | Councils testing more than minimal support |
| Incidents and safeguarding referrals | Commissioners, licensing, CQC or Ofsted where relevant |
| Rent and service charge breakdown by line | Council Housing Benefit teams, rent officers |
| Certificates and expiry dates | Councils, HMO licensing, auditors |
| Repairs with reported and completed dates | Residents, councils, Awaab's Law for registered providers |
| Move-out date, reason and destination | Commissioners, strategy reviews |

### Design principles

- Time-stamp everything and keep an audit trail of edits. A record that can be changed silently is weak evidence.
- Make the right thing easy. Session notes that take two minutes on a phone get written the same day.
- Do not let templates write the notes. Pre-filled text that repeats across residents is a red flag in reviews.
- Build exports that match how councils ask for evidence, per resident and per scheme.
- Separate housing management time from support time in the data, so intensive housing management can be evidenced.

### Data protection

Support records often include health, offending history and other special category data under UK GDPR. You need a lawful basis and a condition for processing special category data, role-based access, encryption, retention rules and a data processing agreement between provider and supplier. The Information Commissioner's Office guidance on special category data is the place to start.

### Sensors and smart building tools

Damp and mould sensors, heating monitors and leak detection help registered providers meet Awaab's Law timescales. They only help if alerts reach someone who can act, and the action is recorded. A dashboard nobody checks creates evidence that a landlord knew about a hazard and did nothing.

## Lease-based models

Many managing agents work for investors who lease supported housing to registered providers. The Regulator of Social Housing has warned since 2019 about long, inflation-linked leases that concentrate risk on the provider. If you manage for an investor, understand the lease terms and the provider's financial position, because a provider in difficulty affects residents and the asset alike. Our guide to [leasing a property to a supported housing provider](/guides/leasing-property-to-a-supported-housing-provider/) lists the checks.
