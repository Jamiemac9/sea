If you're thinking about becoming a registered provider of social housing, the regulator's new annual report has the numbers you'll want to see first. It was published on 13 November 2026 and covers the year to 31 March 2026.

## What the report says about registration

The Regulator of Social Housing received 125 new registration applications in 2025-26, up from 117 the year before. Of those, 97 were preliminary applications and 28 were detailed applications.

It approved 13 applicants onto the register, seven for-profit and six not-for-profit. A further 94 applications were cancelled or withdrawn during the year. Don't read that as 94 refusals. Applicants can withdraw at any stage, the regulator closes applications that stall, and the report doesn't break the number down.

## Why so many don't get through

The regulator's guidance for new entrants gives the clues. Registration has two stages. The preliminary stage checks eligibility. The detailed stage is where you show how you meet the registration criteria on your financial situation, constitution and management arrangements, and the onus is on you to prove it.

The guidance says applicants "can sometimes apply too early", before the organisation or its business model is ready. It won't expect to register an applicant whose plan shows more than 12 months before it becomes a provider. And if there's no clearly progressing contact for six months, the application is discontinued. The fee isn't refunded if an application is withdrawn, cancelled or unsuccessful.

## The lease-based point, again

The report repeats the regulator's concern about some lease-based provision of specialised supported housing. It points back to its April 2025 report and its conclusion that there is "generally not enough flexibility in current lease terms". If your model depends on leased homes, it's worth being ready to explain your lease terms to the regulator. Our guide to [leasing a property to a supported housing provider](/guides/leasing-property-to-a-supported-housing-provider/) covers the lease terms to look at.

## Where it stops

Registration with the regulator is a separate process from the supported housing licensing scheme in the draft regulations, which is still a draft and out for consultation until 11:59pm on 10 December 2026. Nothing in the annual report changes that. Our guide to the [Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act](/guides/supported-housing-regulatory-oversight-act/) explains where licensing came from and what the government has decided so far.
