## Monday 2 November: the four-week needs assessment

The planned licence conditions for supported exempt accommodation say each resident needs a needs assessment when they move in, or within four weeks at the latest.

Three things I would check in your files now:

1. Is the assessment dated, and is the date within four weeks of move-in?
2. Was it done with the resident, or copied from the referral? A referral is a starting point. People change and referrals go out of date.
3. Do the priority needs in the assessment show up as goals in the support plan?

If the answer to any of those is "not always", that is the gap to close before licensing starts.

There is a free needs assessment template on ExemptAccommodation.com if you want a starting point. Link in the comments.

## Wednesday 4 November: for landlords leasing to providers

If you are being offered a long lease and a "guaranteed" rent by a supported housing provider, read this first.

Your rent is only as secure as the provider paying it. Five warning signs:

1. A rent well above what similar homes let for locally, with a promise that Housing Benefit will cover it.
2. A provider with no track record, no published accounts or no contact with the council.
3. Pressure to sign quickly, or to choose the residents yourself.
4. A lease that makes you responsible for everything and the provider for nothing.
5. Marketing that sells exempt accommodation as a high-yield investment.

Also check your mortgage lender and insurer allow the letting. Most standard buy-to-let mortgages do not.
