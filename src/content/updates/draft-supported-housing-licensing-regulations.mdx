**The short version**

- The draft regulations were published on 15 October 2026. The consultation runs for 8 weeks and should close around 10 December 2026 [1].
- Every council in England is proposed as a licensing district. Councils get 6 months to set up their schemes, followed by a 3-month transition [1].
- Proposed civil penalties are up to £7,000 alongside an improvement notice, and up to £40,000 for not complying with one [1].
- Every resident's needs must be assessed on moving in or within 4 weeks [1].
- Parliament must approve the regulations before they take effect [2].

## What has been published

The draft regulations use powers in the [Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023](/guides/supported-housing-regulatory-oversight-act/). They follow the government's April 2026 response, which confirmed that councils will run licensing locally, in every area [5].

The consultation is on the detail of the draft regulations, not on whether licensing should happen. If you want to influence the detail, respond before it closes [1].

## Who will need a licence

Licensing scope mirrors "specified accommodation" in the Housing Benefit regulations (s.12 of the Act) [1]. If your residents' rent is paid through Housing Benefit as [exempt or specified accommodation](/guides/specified-accommodation-categories/), assume you are in scope.

Not sure which category your scheme falls into? Start with [What is exempt accommodation?](/guides/what-is-exempt-accommodation/) or run the free [exempt accommodation checker](/tools/exempt-accommodation-checker/). Lease models, CICs and HMOs are covered in our [grey areas guide](/guides/grey-areas-in-exempt-accommodation/).

## The proposed timeline

Under the draft regulations [1]:

1. Every council in England is designated as a licensing district.
2. Councils have 6 months to set up their schemes.
3. A 3-month transition period follows.
4. After that, operating without a licence becomes an offence.

The offence and banning-order provisions start later than the rest of the regulations [1]. We will add firm dates to the [regulation tracker](/regulation-tracker/) once the final regulations are laid. At this stage, any exact go-live date is an estimate.

## What councils will check

Licences are to be granted with a view to the National Supported Housing Standards being met [2]. The April 2026 response revised these standards, for example by removing the Responsible Person Standard [5]. Our guide to [licensing and the national standards](/guides/supported-housing-regulatory-oversight-act/) covers them in detail.

The draft regulations include three practical tests:

- **Needs assessments.** Each resident's needs must be assessed on moving in or within 4 weeks [1]. If you can't show the move-in date and the assessment date side by side, you have a gap. See [needs assessments, support plans and support records](/guides/support-planning-and-needs-assessments/).
- **Inspections.** Councils get a power of entry after giving at least 24 hours' notice [1]. That is a notice period, not a 24-hour inspection deadline.
- **Fees.** Each council sets its own application and annual fees on a cost-recovery basis. There is no national figure [1].

## Penalties and enforcement

The draft sets out an escalating approach [1]:

- warning notices and improvement notices, with deadlines of up to 3 months
- a civil penalty of up to £7,000 alongside an improvement notice
- a civil penalty of up to £40,000 for not complying with an improvement notice
- banning orders, licence revocation, and personal liability for negligent directors

An offence can be prosecuted with a court fine *or* dealt with by a civil penalty, but not both [1]. Licence revocations are reported to the council's Housing Benefit team [1]. So losing a licence could affect your rent income as well as your registration. For how eligible rent is tested, see [rent, service charges and Housing Benefit](/guides/housing-benefit-rent-and-service-charges/).

## Property safety: what licensing does not change

The draft licensing regulations do not replace existing safety law. Gas safety checks are still required every 12 months [3]. Electrical installation condition reports (EICRs) are required at least every 5 years where the private rented sector electrical regulations apply [4]. Whether those regulations apply depends on the tenancy or licence your residents hold, so check each scheme. Every rule is set out in one table in [property standards and safety](/guides/property-standards-and-safety/).

## How to prepare now

You don't need to wait for the final regulations. Most of the evidence a licensing officer will want is already tested in Housing Benefit reviews.

1. **List every scheme by council area.** Each council will run its own scheme and set its own fees.
2. **Check every resident's assessment date against their move-in date.** Fix anything that falls outside 4 weeks.
3. **Put certificates in one place,** with issue and expiry dates recorded rather than buried in PDFs.
4. **Make your records hold up.** Assessments and plans should be signed and dated, and you should be able to see who changed what.
5. **Brief your board** on the proposed penalties, director liability and the link to Housing Benefit.
6. **Respond to the consultation** if the detail affects how you operate [1].
