## Quick brief

- **The exemption.** A tenancy that meets supported exempt accommodation in section 12 of the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023 does not have to go on the Register your rental property service [1].
- **The rollout.** Registration opens region by region. The West Midlands period starts on 15 December 2026. The East of England follows on 15 January 2027 [1].
- **The fee.** Properties that have to register pay £65 a year. That fee does not apply to a section 12 tenancy [1].
- **RSH grades.** On 28 October 2026 the regulator gave Haringey Council a C4 and Tamworth and Woking councils a C3, with overdue fire and health and safety actions among the findings [3].

## A practical exemption, tenancy by tenancy

On 29 October 2026 the government published guidance for the Register your rental property service. The Renters' Rights Act requires private landlords in England, including organisations, to register themselves and their privately let homes [1].

The service opens by region. Each region has a three-month window. The West Midlands runs from 15 December 2026 to 14 March 2027, and the East of England from 15 January 2027 to 14 April 2027 [1]. You can register before your region's start date. You must finish by the end date.

The annual fee is £65 per property, with no VAT. During the rollout the first bill is lower, because the charge is worked out only from 15 November 2027. The guidance's own example says a property registered on 15 December 2026 would pay £5.34 for that first period, then £65 a year [1].

You do not need to register a tenancy that falls within supported exempt accommodation in [section 12 of the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/26/section/12) [1] [2]. Section 12 points back to the Housing Benefit categories, including managed properties, refuges and local authority hostels. The [regulatory oversight guide](/guides/supported-housing-regulatory-oversight-act/) sets out how that line is drawn.

The exemption is decided tenancy by tenancy. A provider that also lets ordinary assured tenancies still has to register those homes. The [Renters' Rights Act guide](/guides/renters-rights-act-supported-accommodation/) sets out which agreements the Act already covers.

If the care, support or supervision is too thin to meet section 12, the tenancy is back in the register. Failing to register can draw a penalty of up to £7,000. Knowingly or recklessly giving false or misleading information can draw a penalty of up to £40,000 [1]. The admin and the fee stay away only when you can evidence the support.

## The regulator is not easing off on safety

The register exemption does not change the Regulator of Social Housing's consumer standards. Those standards apply to registered providers.

On 27 October 2026 the regulator published its stock and rents statistics. Will Perry, Director of Strategy, said good quality data is essential to making the right strategic decisions for residents and the long-term health of organisations [4]. The same release puts supported housing at 11% of the 4.6 million social homes owned by registered providers [4].

On 28 October the regulator published judgements for 15 landlords. Haringey Council received a C4, the lowest consumer grade. The inspection found almost 4,000 overdue fire safety remedial actions, including more than 1,400 classed as high risk, and a large repairs backlog that included damp and mould [3].

Tamworth Borough Council and Woking Borough Council each received a C3. Tamworth had not shown that actions from health and safety assessments were completed in time. Woking had weaknesses in overdue fire, water and asbestos remedial actions [3].

These grades are about council landlords, not a separate inspection of every exempt accommodation provider. They still matter if you are a registered provider and your stock includes supported housing. The regulator says it may act where standards are breached and there is a significant risk of serious detriment to tenants [4].

A charity or community interest company that is not a registered provider will not receive a C grade. Councils will still expect the same safety file when licensing starts, and when they check Housing Benefit. The [property standards guide](/guides/property-standards-and-safety/) is the practical list, and the [regulatory oversight guide](/guides/supported-housing-regulatory-oversight-act/) covers how licensing will test it.

## Frequently asked questions

**When does the Renters' Rights property register launch?**

The service opens by region, starting in the West Midlands on 15 December 2026. That window closes on 14 March 2027. The East of England starts on 15 January 2027. London is last in this sequence, from 15 July 2027 [1].

**Do I have to pay the £65 property registration fee for exempt accommodation?**

No, where the tenancy meets supported exempt accommodation in section 12 of the 2023 Act. You do not register that tenancy, and you do not pay the fee for it [1]. An ordinary assured tenancy in the same building is still in scope.
