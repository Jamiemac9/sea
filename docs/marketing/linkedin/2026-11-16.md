## Monday 16 November: Gold, Green, Amber, Red

Every resident file we audit gets one of four ratings: Gold, Green, Amber or Red.

Amber is not failure. In the example on our website, everything in the file was in order apart from one 6-month review, completed 15 days late. One action to take, and three strengths named alongside it.

That is the point of rating every file the same way. A Registered Provider overseeing several managing agents can compare them like for like, and a managing agent can see exactly which files to fix first.

Every finding names the document and the date. If something is not in the file, we record it as not seen.

If you want to know where your files would land, a Mock Audit of a sample is the place to start. Link in the comments.

## Wednesday 18 November: intensive housing management

Intensive housing management is one of the most challenged lines in a supported housing service charge.

It can be eligible for Housing Benefit, but only if your evidence shows it is housing management and not support. That usually means a time record for any staff whose time is split between the two.

If a council asks, you want to show:

- what the housing management tasks are
- who does them and for how long
- how that time is kept separate from support
- how the charge is calculated from it

## Friday 20 November: who is really the landlord?

A grey area that catches out lease models.

For exempt accommodation, the landlord is the organisation that grants the occupancy agreement. So a private owner can lease a property to a charity, and the charity becomes the landlord.

Where it goes wrong: if the owner chooses the residents, sets the rents or runs the scheme day to day, a council may question whether the charity is really the landlord at all.

The fix is simple to say and harder to do. Make sure the lease and the management agreement match what actually happens. The landlord should set policy, approve rents, control who moves in and hold the relationship with residents.
