  - q: Is supported housing exempt from HMO licensing?
    a: Only if the building is managed or controlled by a body listed in Schedule 14 to the Housing Act 2004, such as a local housing authority or a registered provider of social housing. Supported houses run by charities or community interest companies are not exempt on that basis.
  - q: Does Awaab's Law apply to supported housing?
    a: It applies to social housing let by registered providers under a tenancy, which includes much supported and temporary accommodation they let. The first phase, in force since 27 October 2025, covers emergency hazards and significant damp and mould. Later phases extend it to other hazards.
  - q: Who is responsible for fire safety in a supported shared house?
    a: The responsible person under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, usually the landlord or the organisation in control of the premises. They must carry out a fire risk assessment for the common parts and act on it. Many councils use national fire safety guidance for HMOs when deciding what is adequate.
  - q: Do I need an EPC for a supported housing room?
    a: Rules on energy performance certificates depend on how the property is let. Whole self-contained homes let on tenancies normally need one. For rooms in shared houses the position is more complex, so check current guidance for your property type.

When the government responded to its supported housing consultation in April 2026, it said it would not create new accommodation standards for supported housing. That is not a relaxation. It means the existing rules, which already apply, will be enforced alongside licensing.

## The rules at a glance

| Rule | Applies to | What it requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Housing Health and Safety Rating System | All residential property | No serious hazards. Councils can enforce. |
| Mandatory HMO licensing | Five or more people, two or more households, sharing facilities, unless managed by an exempt body | A licence from the council, with conditions |
| Additional and selective licensing | Areas the council designates | A licence for smaller HMOs or all private lets in the area |
| HMO management regulations | All HMOs, licensed or not | Duties on repairs, fire safety, waste, common parts |
| Fire safety order | Common parts of HMOs and blocks | Fire risk assessment by the responsible person |
| Gas safety regulations | Properties with gas appliances | Annual check by a Gas Safe engineer |
| Electrical safety regulations | Private rented homes | Inspection at least every five years |
| Smoke and CO alarm regulations | Private and social rented homes | Smoke alarm on every storey, CO alarm with fixed combustion appliances |
| Awaab's Law | Registered providers' social housing tenancies | Fixed timescales to deal with hazards |
| Decent Homes Standard | Social housing | Home in reasonable repair, with modern facilities and warmth |

## Housing Health and Safety Rating System

The system, under Part 1 of the Housing Act 2004, applies to all residential premises. It covers 29 hazards, from damp and mould to falls and fire. If a council finds a serious hazard, it must act. Supported housing gets no exemption, and residents with health conditions can be more vulnerable to some hazards.

## HMO licensing

A mandatory HMO licence is needed for houses with five or more people from two or more households sharing kitchens, bathrooms or toilets. Councils can add additional licensing for smaller HMOs and selective licensing for whole areas.

Schedule 14 to the Housing Act 2004 takes buildings managed or controlled by a local housing authority, a registered provider of social housing, the police, fire services or certain health bodies outside the HMO licensing rules. Charities and community interest companies are not on that list, so their shared supported houses usually need licences.

## Fire safety

The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 applies to the common parts of HMOs and blocks of flats. The responsible person must carry out a fire risk assessment, act on it and keep it under review. In supported housing, assessments should take account of residents' needs, such as whether they can respond to an alarm or evacuate unaided.

## Gas, electrical and alarms

- Gas appliances, fittings and flues need an annual safety check by a Gas Safe registered engineer, with a copy of the record given to residents.
- Private landlords must have electrical installations inspected and tested at least every five years. Registered providers are covered by separate social housing requirements.
- Since 1 October 2022, private and social landlords must fit a smoke alarm on every storey used as living accommodation and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance other than a gas cooker.
- A legionella risk assessment is good practice for any property with stored hot water.

## Awaab's Law

Awaab's Law came into force for social housing on 27 October 2025. It applies to almost all social housing let by registered providers under a tenancy, including temporary and supported accommodation. The first phase requires landlords to make emergency hazards safe within 24 hours and to investigate significant damp and mould hazards within fixed timescales. Later phases bring in other hazards.

Providers that are not registered providers are not covered by Awaab's Law, but the Housing Health and Safety Rating System still applies to them, and councils can enforce it.

## Building a compliance tracker

For every property, keep one record of:

1. Address, landlord, managing agent and responsible person for fire safety.
2. HMO licence status, licence number, conditions and expiry.
3. Gas safety record date and next due date.
4. Electrical inspection date and next due date.
5. Fire risk assessment date, actions and next review.
6. Alarm test records.
7. Open repairs, with dates reported and fixed.

A tracker like this is what managing agents, auditors and licensing officers will ask for first.
