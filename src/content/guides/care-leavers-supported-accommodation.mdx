Young people leaving care are one of the groups supported housing exists for. They are also the group where the rules changed most sharply in the last five years, and where getting it wrong is now a criminal offence.

## The rules change at 16, 18 and 25

| Age | What applies | Who pays for accommodation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 16 | Must be in a registered children's home or foster care. Unregulated placements banned since 9 September 2021. | Children's services |
| 16 and 17 | Supported accommodation must be registered with Ofsted if the young person is looked after or a care leaver | Children's services, not Housing Benefit |
| 18 to 20 | Adult housing rules apply. Staying Put possible with former foster carers. | Universal Credit or Housing Benefit, with council support |
| 21 to 24 | Personal adviser support continues to 25 on request | Universal Credit or Housing Benefit. Shared accommodation rate exemption applies. |
| 25 and over | Care leaver status mostly ends for benefit purposes | Standard rules |

## Ofsted registration for 16 and 17 year olds

The Supported Accommodation (England) Regulations 2023 brought supported accommodation for looked-after 16 and 17 year olds and care leavers into regulation. Ofsted started taking applications in April 2023, and registration became mandatory on 28 October 2023. Running this kind of accommodation without registration is now an offence.

Registered providers must meet four quality standards:

1. Leadership and management
2. Protection
3. Accommodation
4. Support

Providers register for the type of accommodation they offer, such as single occupancy, shared accommodation or supported lodgings, and Ofsted inspects against the standards. Ofsted planned to begin inspections from April 2024.

<Callout type="watch" title="If you are a landlord, not a provider">
Ofsted registration sits with the organisation providing the supported accommodation, not the owner of the building. If you lease property to a young people's provider, check its registration covers your address and keep evidence of that check. The property still has to meet the accommodation standard.
</Callout>

## Homeless 16 and 17 year olds who are not in care

A 16 or 17 year old who becomes homeless may not be in care yet. Following the House of Lords decision in the Southwark case in 2009, children's services should assess them as a child in need and may need to accommodate them under section 20 of the Children Act 1989, which makes them looked after. Housing teams should not simply place them in adult hostels. If you get a direct referral of a homeless 17 year old, ask the council whether children's services has assessed them.

## Who pays at 16 and 17

Care leavers aged 16 and 17 are excluded from Housing Benefit and Universal Credit housing costs under the Children (Leaving Care) Act 2000. The council's children's services must provide or pay for their accommodation and maintenance. In practice that means a contract or spot purchase arrangement with the provider, not a Housing Benefit claim.

When the young person turns 18, the funding switches. Plan for this months in advance: start the Universal Credit claim, check whether the accommodation will qualify as specified accommodation, and agree with children's services what happens in the gap.

## From 18 to 25

Care leavers keep some extra help as adults:

- A personal adviser up to age 25 if they want one.
- The shared accommodation rate exemption up to 25, in force since 31 May 2021, so they can claim the one-bedroom Local Housing Allowance rate in private rented housing.
- Many councils offer council tax relief for care leavers, often to 25. Check the local offer.
- Staying Put with former foster carers up to 21.
- Staying Close support, which many councils now offer, for young people leaving residential children's homes.

Care leavers often have to tell the DWP or the council about their status themselves. Building that step into move-in paperwork saves money for the young person and avoids underpayments.

## How licensing treats young people's schemes

The Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023 licensing regime is aimed at supported exempt accommodation. In its April 2026 response, the government said Ofsted-regulated schemes serving young people up to 25 will be exempt from licensing where at least one bed in the scheme is regulated by Ofsted. That avoids double regulation of the same building.

Schemes for young adults aged 18 to 25 with no Ofsted-regulated beds are expected to need a licence once the regime starts. Providers who think they are exempt must notify the council, which can disagree, with a right of appeal.

## Good practice in young people's services

- Plan around education, training and work from the first week, not only housing.
- Keep a clear record of contact with the young person's social worker or personal adviser.
- Make safeguarding procedures specific to under-18s, including missing-from-home protocols and exploitation risks.
- Prepare for the 18th birthday at least three months ahead, including the benefit claim and any change to the occupancy agreement.
- Ask young people what works. Ofsted inspectors will.
