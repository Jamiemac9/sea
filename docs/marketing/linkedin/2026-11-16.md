## Monday 16 November: Gold, Green, Amber, Red

Amber isn't a fail. That's the first thing I'd want anyone to know about how a Support Audit rates a resident file.

Every file gets one of four ratings: Gold, Green, Amber or Red, and each one comes with a reason.

Take the example file on our website. The licence agreement, the support needs assessment, the risk plans and the weekly support all pass. One 6-month review was done 15 days after it was due. That's Amber: one action, with three strengths named alongside it.

Rating every file against the same key is what makes the results useful. A Registered Provider can compare files, managing agents and rounds side by side, and a managing agent can see which files to fix first.

Every finding names the document and the date. If something isn't in the file, it's recorded as not seen. Nothing is assumed.

Two things you could start this week, whoever checks your files:

- Put the next review due date on the front page of every support plan, so a late review gets spotted before it happens, not after.
- When a review does run late, record why and the date it was finally done. A late review with a reason reads very differently from one that's simply missing.

What key does your team use to compare files across schemes?

## Tuesday 17 November: missed support sessions

A missed support session is still part of the support record. Four questions that come up about it.

Does a missed session count against the file?
Not on its own. People are at work, at appointments, unwell, or not ready to talk that week. What counts is whether the file shows what happened next.

What should the note say?
Log it the same day as an entry in its own right: the planned time, who was going, and what was tried. "No answer at 2pm, card left, text sent" is a record. A blank week isn't.

How many missed sessions before someone reviews it?
That's your call, but write the number into your procedure and use it every time. Then the review is a short dated entry: what's been tried, what's changed, what happens next.

Does any of this come from the rules?
The proposed person-centred support standard in the government's consultation response says "there must be procedure in place to encourage the resident's engagement with their support and/or supervision". The standards aren't published or in force yet, so this is good practice for now, not a requirement.

Two things you could start this week:

- Add a "missed session" entry to your notes template with three fields: planned time, what was tried, next attempt.
- Pull five files and list every gap of more than a week. Tick off the ones the notes explain.

I've put a worked example of a weak and a strong record in a new guide. Link in the comments.

## Wednesday 18 November: intensive housing management

Intensive housing management is one of the most challenged lines in a supported housing service charge.

It can be eligible for Housing Benefit, but only if your evidence shows it is housing management and not support. That usually means a time record for any staff whose time is split between the two.

If a council asks, you want to show:

- what the housing management tasks are
- who does them and for how long
- how that time is kept separate from support
- how the charge is calculated from it

## Friday 20 November: who is really the landlord?

A grey area that catches out lease models.

For exempt accommodation, the landlord is the organisation that grants the occupancy agreement. So a private owner can lease a property to a charity, and the charity becomes the landlord.

Where it goes wrong: if the owner chooses the residents, sets the rents or runs the scheme day to day, a council may question whether the charity is really the landlord at all.

The fix is simple to say and harder to do. Make sure the lease and the management agreement match what actually happens. The landlord should set policy, approve rents, control who moves in and hold the relationship with residents.
