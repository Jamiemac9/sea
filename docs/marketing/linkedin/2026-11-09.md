## Monday 9 November: session notes that pass an audit

The session note is where most support files are won or lost.

A note that says "Met with resident, all fine" tells an auditor nothing. A note that passes has:

- the date, start and end time
- who was there
- what you did, in a sentence or two
- which support plan goal it relates to
- any risks or concerns raised
- the next action, and who owns it

That link to a plan goal is the one most often missing. Without it, there is no way to show the support matches the resident's needs.

## Wednesday 11 November: the training matrix

No single law lists the training supported housing staff must have. The duties come from several places:

- Fire safety training is a legal duty for every member of staff, at induction and whenever the risks change.
- Registered Providers must meet the Competence and Conduct Standard, which started last month.
- CQC-registered services must train staff in learning disability and autism.
- Licensing is expected to check that service managers have suitable skills and experience.

A certificate is not proof of competence. What councils look for is a training matrix with dates, and supervision notes that show staff can apply it.

## Friday 13 November: the dates that matter

The dates every supported housing provider in England should have in the diary:

- Later in 2026: draft licensing regulations and guidance are due to be consulted on.
- 31 March 2027: the current planned deadline for councils to publish their first supported housing strategies.
- 2027: councils are expected to start licensing supported exempt accommodation.

Dates move. I keep a regulation tracker updated, and you can get an email when anything on it changes. Link in the comments.
